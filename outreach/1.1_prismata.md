**Subject:** Question about the §3 critical-path measurement in Prismata (arXiv:2607.08147)

Dear Corban, Alp Eren, Sijun and Professor Popa,

I am Mohammad Alam, and I am running a measurement study of structural defenses against prompt injection in web agents, Prismata among them. We tried to recompute your §3 statistic ("Only 1,086 of the 90,408 untrusted paths (1.2%) contain an actionable descendant") on Mind2Web pages. Our result moves a lot with choices the paper leaves open, so we would rather ask than guess.

1. **The unit.** What is one "untrusted path instance": one per DOM node your provenance model flags, one per maximal untrusted region, or something else? On our pages the same criterion gives 1.6% to 19.6% depending on the unit.
2. **The page representation for the Mind2Web half.** Did you use the `raw_html` or `cleaned_html` from the Hugging Face release, the MHTML snapshots, or pages re-rendered in a browser? The released `raw_html` keeps 21 attribute names and has no `href`, `onclick`, `tabindex` or `contenteditable` on any of the 57 sites we checked. That changes what can count as a link or an onclick handler.
3. **The link rule.** Does "a link" mean any `<a>` element, or an `<a>` with an `href`? Was actionability computed from the HTML text or in a browser through DevTools?
4. **The split.** How do the 1,086 critical paths and the 94 Case-3 paths divide between Common Crawl and Mind2Web?
5. **Data.** Could you share the list of the 5,664 DOMs, or your per-node provenance labels for a subset? Even 100 Mind2Web pages would let us compute our statistics on your labels instead of ours.

We will send you our results before any public version, and we would be glad to talk if that is easier than email.

Best regards,
Mohammad Alam
