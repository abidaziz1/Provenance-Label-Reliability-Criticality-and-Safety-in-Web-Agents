**What F3 means for the candidates.** Prismata defines a critical path for every interactive element, not for the task target. An untrusted node U lies on the root-to-E chain of some element E exactly when E is a descendant of U. So "lay on a critical path to an interactable element" (§1.1), the §2.2 definition, and "contain an actionable descendant" (§3) all state the same definition. The paper never states a strict root-to-task-target reading. C1 has to be framed as "the published number does not reproduce under the paper's only stated definition", not as "the paper mixes two definitions". The action gate described in §2.2 does not use the task target, which weakens C2 (see Section 4).

## 3. C1: the 1.2% does not reproduce under Prismata's stated definition

**Candidate as written:** Prismata's 1.2% matches the strict root-to-task-target reading (we measure 1.13% to 1.73% on 57 Mind2Web sites), not the "contains an actionable descendant" reading its Sec. 3 states (we measure 49.93%): a forty-fold definitional gap.

**Corrections from the fact check.**
- The paper states one definition, three times (§1.1, §2.2, §3), and the three are equivalent (F3). No task-target reading appears in the text. The gap is between the published number and what that stated definition gives on our data. That Prismata computed a task-target version is our hypothesis for how the published number arose.
- Mind2Web supplies 72.4% of the pool (65,416 of 90,408); Common Crawl supplies the other 27.6% (24,992) (F5).

**Arithmetic that makes C1 stronger** (my derivation from the verified counts):
- Suppose all 1,086 critical paths came from Mind2Web. The Mind2Web rate would still be at most 1,086 / 65,416 = **1.66%**. Our measurement under the stated definition is 49.93%, about 30 times that ceiling.
- Apply our 49.93% to Prismata's 65,416 Mind2Web instances. That alone gives about 32,660 critical paths, 36.1% of the whole pool, even if Common Crawl contributed none.
- So the mix of corpora cannot explain the gap. Only a difference in the unit of measurement, the labeler, or the definition can.
- Our task-target range (1.13% to 1.73%) contains both the published 1.2% and the 1.66% ceiling.
