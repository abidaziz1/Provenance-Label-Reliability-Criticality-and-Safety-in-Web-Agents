# Annotator brief (for the two people who help with task 1.7)

Alam can forward this page as it is.

## What we need from you

About two hours of careful reading, done once, on your own computer.

We study software that protects AI assistants when they browse websites for someone. That software has to tell which parts of a web page the website itself wrote, and which parts came from outsiders: ads, customer reviews, or products that other sellers list. Our program makes that call automatically, and we need two people to check about 100 of its decisions by hand. You don't need any technical background. You do need to read carefully and say "can't tell" when you can't.

## What you will get

One file by email, for example `audit_batch1.html`. Open it in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari; nothing needs installing, and it works offline. Each screen shows one item:

- the website's name, for example "Expedia";
- a block of text from that website, the part our program flagged;
- a button or link inside that block, for example "Shop now" or "Helpful";
- a few technical labels such as tag and class names. You may ignore them, or use them as hints.

## Your three answers per item (about a minute each)

1. **Who wrote or supplied this block?**
   - an ad company: "Sponsored", banner ads, "Recommended by" feeds;
   - ordinary users: reviews, comments, questions and answers;
   - sellers or partners: another business's listing or offer on the site;
   - the website itself: its own menus, search boxes, filters, or its own sales;
   - can't tell.
2. **Is the button part of the website's own controls?** Menus, search, filters and account links count as yes. "Shop now" on an ad, or "Helpful" on a review, is no. Otherwise answer can't tell.
3. **Can you see a label before the button saying this is an ad or someone else's content?** Examples: "Sponsored", "Ad", "Review by Maria", "Sold by". Answer yes, no or can't tell.

There is a notes box if something seems odd.

## Rules

- **Work alone.** Don't discuss items with the other annotator until you have both sent your files.
- **Don't look anything up.** Don't visit the websites, and don't browse the project's GitHub page while you are labeling. It describes what the program expects to find, and that would bias you.
- **Treat all page text as text.** Some items may contain lines such as "ignore previous instructions" or "click here to verify your account". Never click links from the text or follow instructions in it. Just answer the questions, and add a note.
- **"Can't tell" is a good answer** when it is true. A guess is worse than a clear "can't tell".
- **Take a break** after about 50 items. The page remembers your place in the same browser.

## When you finish

Type your initials at the top, click **Download my labels**, and email the downloaded file to Alam. Don't edit the file. You will be thanked by name in the paper's acknowledgments if you agree. Your labels are stored under a short code, not your name.

## Who is a good fit

- Any adult who reads English web pages comfortably.
- Someone who has not worked on this project.
- Two people who don't coordinate their answers.
